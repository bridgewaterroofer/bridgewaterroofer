COMMERCIAL ROOFING VULNERABILITIES

8 Critical Commercial Roof Problems We Diagnose & Fix

Commercial flat roofs face intense structural, environmental, and thermal stresses. We provide permanent engineering remedies for every commercial roof defect:

Persistent Ponding Water

Water lingering past 48 hours accelerates UV degradation, breaks down lap adhesives, and adds hazardous dead weight to commercial steel joists.

Open Seams & Heat-Weld Splits

Thermal contraction pulls aged single-ply seams apart, allowing pressurized rainwater directly into underlying polyisocyanurate insulation boards.

Rooftop HVAC Curb Flashing Leaks

Vibration from commercial mechanical units loosens target patches and pitch pockets, funneling water into air duct returns and interior suites.

Membrane Punctures & Foot Traffic Tears

Frequent HVAC servicing, dropped tools, and windblown rooftop gravel create hidden punctures through single-ply TPO or EPDM membranes.

Blistering & Entrapped Moisture

Vapor expansion beneath plies creates gas blisters in summer heat. Ruptured blisters allow moisture into the entire commercial roof substrate.

Parapet Wall & Coping Metal Failures

Unsealed coping caps, loose termination bars, and failing masonry reglet flashing allow wind-driven rain behind vertical membrane turn-ups.

Thermal Shock & Membrane Shrinkage

Decades of Bridgewater freeze-thaw cycles cause unreinforced rubber membranes to shrink, pulling bridging away from perimeter angle changes.

Wind Uplift & Edge Metal Peel

Severe highway wind funnels along Route 22 catch loose fascia drip edges, peeling commercial membranes and exposing the deck to heavy rain.

EARLY DETECTION PROTOCOL

8 Warning Signs Your Commercial Roof Demands Attention

Catching commercial moisture ingress early prevents expensive tenant claims, inventory losses, electrical outages, and deck rust:

Ceiling Tile Water Rings in Offices

Stained, discolored, or dripping acoustical ceiling tiles inside tenant spaces indicate saturated polyisocyanurate insulation directly above.

Standing Ponding Beyond 48 Hours

Water pooling near center bays indicates compressed insulation, structural deck deflection, or undersized drainage bowls.

Rising Facility HVAC Operating Costs

Saturated commercial roof insulation loses up to 80% of its thermal resistance, forcing rooftop cooling compressors to run continuously.

Loose or Detached Parapet Termination

Gaps between the commercial membrane termination bar and exterior parapet masonry allow driving wind-driven rain behind walls.

Visible Seam Separation & Fishmouths

Open voids or curling edges along membrane overlap seams allow rainwater to siphon underneath during torrential thunderstorms.

Soft or Spongy Decking Underfoot

Walking the roof reveals spongy spots where trapped moisture has rotted wood nailers or corroded corrugated steel sub-decking.

Musty Odors Near Air Handler Intakes

Moisture festering in wet commercial insulation breeds mold, which gets pulled into fresh-air intakes and circulated to tenants.

Cracked Pitch Pockets Around Conduits

Deteriorated pourable sealants around electrical conduits, plumbing penetrations, and AC line-sets allow direct water ingress.

TRANSPARENT ESTIMATION

8 Factors That Determine Commercial Roofing Investment

Commercial facilities have unique architectural and logistical variables. Here are the genuine technical cost drivers that determine commercial project scopes:

01

Membrane Mil Thickness

Choosing an 80-mil TPO membrane instead of 45-mil doubles puncture resistance and extends NDL manufacturer warranty eligibility up to 30 years.

02

NJ Energy Code & Tapered Polyiso

New Jersey UCC mandates minimum continuous R-30 insulation on commercial re-roofs. Designing tapered polyiso crickets to direct water toward drains influences material volume.

03

Existing Layers (NJ 2-Layer Rule)

Under N.J.A.C. 5:23, a roof with two existing layers cannot receive a third overlay. A full tear-off to the deck is legally required, including disposal tonnage.

04

Rooftop HVAC Curb Density

A 20,000 sq ft flat roof with 25 mechanical units, skylights, and pipe clusters requires significantly more hand labor and custom flashing than an open warehouse roof.

05

Building Height & Crane Access

Multi-story office buildings or facilities with tight property setbacks require specialized boom cranes, street closure permits, and complex material staging logistics.

06

Attachment: Adhered vs Mechanically Fastened

Fully adhered systems utilize commercial bonding adhesives for seamless wind resistance and aesthetic smoothness, while mechanically attached systems use induction plates.

07

Manufacturer NDL Warranty Tier

Obtaining a certified No Dollar Limit (NDL) warranty from Carlisle or GAF requires manufacturer plan reviews, certified details, and independent factory field inspections.

08

Phased Off-Hours Scheduling

Projects requiring low-odor adhesives, off-hours night/weekend shifts, or phased safety zones to maintain retail shopping operations require specialized project management.

ANSWERS TO COMMON QUESTIONS

Commercial Roofing FAQs

Straightforward answers to questions commercial facility managers and property owners ask when planning roofing projects in Bridgewater:

What is an NDL warranty and why is it critical for commercial properties? +

An NDL ("No Dollar Limit") warranty is issued directly by commercial roofing manufacturers (such as Carlisle SynTec or GAF Commercial) rather than an individual contractor. Under an NDL warranty, there is no monetary cap on repair costs if the roof leaks due to material defects or certified installation workmanship over the 20- or 30-year warranty term. This provides total balance-sheet protection for commercial property owners.

Can we replace our commercial roof while our business remains open? +

Yes. Over 90% of our commercial roofing projects are performed with zero tenant interruption. We use low-odor cold adhesives or induction plate fastening instead of smelly asphalt kettles, erect covered pedestrian safety tunnels at all building entrances, coordinate designated crane staging zones, and offer off-hours early-morning or weekend shifts for noise-sensitive businesses like medical plazas and corporate offices.

Which membrane is superior for Bridgewater's climate: TPO or EPDM? +

Both are proven systems with distinct strengths. White TPO excels in solar heat reflection, reducing summer commercial HVAC air conditioning costs by up to 25%, and features robotic hot-air welded seams that are virtually impervious to ponding water. Black EPDM synthetic rubber remains exceptionally flexible down to -49°F, absorbs winter solar heat to melt snow loads faster, and has a 50+ year proven service track record in the Northeast. We evaluate your building's thermal envelope and roof layout to recommend the optimal match.

Does New Jersey Uniform Construction Code permit a roof overlay? +

Under N.J.A.C. 5:23 and the International Building Code (IBC 2021 NJ Edition), a commercial roof may only have a maximum of two roofing layers. If your building currently has one layer that is structurally sound and free of saturated moisture, an overlay system may be permitted. However, if your building already has two layers or if extensive insulation is wet, a complete tear-off down to the structural deck is legally mandated.

How does a fluid-applied silicone coating differ from a full replacement? +

A fluid-applied silicone restoration is a sustainable alternative that coats an existing, dry roof membrane with high-solids silicone. It creates a 100% seamless, monolithic waterproof layer that resists permanent ponding water. It eliminates landfill disposal fees, costs 40% to 60% less than a full tear-off, and provides a 10- to 20-year manufacturer warranty. Furthermore, coatings can often be expensed in full in the year applied rather than capitalized over 39 years.

Are commercial roof replacements tax deductible under Section 179? +

Under the Tax Cuts and Jobs Act (TCJA), Section 179 of the IRS tax code allows qualifying commercial building owners to expense the full cost of commercial roof improvements (replacements, coatings, and major retrofits) up to allowable limits in the year of installation, rather than depreciating the asset over 39 years. We recommend consulting your CPA or tax advisor to verify your exact deduction eligibility.

What causes ponding water on commercial flat roofs and how do you fix it? +

Ponding occurs when water remains on a flat roof for more than 48 hours without draining. Primary causes include inadequate original slope, compressed or waterlogged insulation boards, undersized or clogged internal drain bowls, or settling structural purlins. We resolve ponding by designing custom tapered polyisocyanurate insulation crickets, installing drain sumps, or cutting new perimeter through-wall scuppers.

How often should our commercial property undergo a roof inspection? +

Commercial roof warranties typically require documented bi-annual inspections: once in the Spring to identify freeze-thaw damage, and once in the Autumn to clean drain baskets before winter snowfall. Additionally, inspections should be conducted immediately following any severe storm event, hurricane, or heavy rooftop HVAC mechanical service.

What insurance and safety certifications does Bridgewater Roofer maintain? +

Bridgewater Roofer carries $5,000,000 in comprehensive commercial general liability insurance, full statutory workers' compensation, and completed operations coverage. Our field supervisors are OSHA 30 certified, and all commercial roof technicians undergo continuous fall-protection and equipment safety training.

How do you handle commercial emergency roof leaks during severe weather? +

Our 24/7 commercial emergency response unit mobilizes within 90 minutes anywhere in Somerset County. We carry specialized wet-surface commercial mastic, high-tack rubber emergency patches, weighted containment tarps, and internal water diversion funnels to protect inventory, servers, and tenant spaces while rain continues. Once conditions dry, we return to perform permanent heat-welded membrane repairs.
