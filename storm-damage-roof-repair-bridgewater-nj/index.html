DAMAGE TYPES

Types of Storm Damage We Repair

Somerset County storms produce distinct types of roofing trauma. Here are the primary storm damages we diagnose and repair:

Wind-Lifted & Creased Shingles

Gale winds break thermal seal strips and bend tabs backward, creating horizontal crease fractures across shingles that cause persistent water leakage.

Hail Bruising & Punctures

Hail impacts crush mineral granules into the asphalt core or punch small divots through the fiberglass mat, stripping UV defense.

Fallen Tree Branch Damage

Heavy limbs falling from mature Bridgewater tree canopies punch holes through plywood sheathing and shatter surrounding shingle courses.

Dislodged Ridge Caps

Peak ridge caps endure the highest aerodynamic lift. Severe windstorms frequently rip caps completely off the peak, exposing open ridge vents.

Torn Flashing & Wall Seams

Microburst gusts peel unseated aluminum counter-flashing away from brick chimneys, dormers, and sidewalls, allowing rainwater to pour behind siding.

Gutter Detachment & Ice Dams

Snow overload and wind-driven rain tear gutters away from fascia boards, pulling drip edges and opening direct routes for moisture behind exterior walls.

Damaged Roof Vents & Stacks

Hail bruises aluminum box vents and high winds rattle vent caps loose, creating unsealed nail holes where rain trickles directly down attic framing.

Valley Metal Dislodgement

High-volume runoff during torrential storms buckles unfastened valley metal, forcing rushing water backward under neighboring shingles.

POST-STORM AUDIT

Warning Signs of Roof Storm Damage

After high winds, heavy rain, or hail sweep through Bridgewater, inspect your property for these telltale signs:

Dented Gutters & Downspouts

Small round dings or dimples in aluminum gutters, downspouts, and mailbox tops signal hail impact on the roof shingles above.

Shingle Pieces or Granules in Yard

Finding severed shingle tabs on your lawn or piles of asphalt grit at downspout splash blocks indicates severe wind shear on the roof.

Horizontal Crease Lines on Shingles

Wind-lifted shingles flap up and down during gales, creating thin black crease fractures across the top tab where seal strips detached.

New Ceiling Water Spots After Rain

Fresh brown water rings or wet drywall appearing after a storm indicate wind has forced water through unsealed nail penetrations.

Dented Air Conditioner Fin Housing

Check your outdoor AC condenser unit. Dented heat fins and metal tops provide official forensic evidence of hail size for insurance.

Twisted or Loose Flashing Aprons

High winds pry flashing loose around chimneys and sidewalls, breaking waterproof seals without dislodging full shingles.

Cracked or Punctured Skylight Domes

Hailstones fracture acrylic and glass skylight caps, creating spiderweb cracks that allow rain to seep around perimeter trims.

Neighbors Filing Roof Storm Claims

Storm cells strike entire subdivisions uniformly. If your neighbors are meeting with insurance adjusters, your roof likely sustained damage too.

OBJECTIVE EVALUATION

Spot Storm Repair vs. Insurance Replacement

Depending on wind speeds, hail size, and damage distribution, insurance criteria dictate whether spot repairs or full replacement are justified:

SPOT STORM REPAIR WORKS WHEN:

Spot Repair Makes Sense When:

  • ✔Wind damage is confined to a single facet with fewer than 5–8 blown shingles.
  • ✔Surrounding shingles remain pliable and can be lifted without tearing.
  • ✔Existing shingle color and manufacturer lines are readily available in local stock.
  • ✔The estimated repair cost is below your insurance policy deductible.
Swift localized repair avoids insurance premium hikes and restores watertight protection.
INSURANCE REPLACEMENT QUALIFIES WHEN:

Full Replacement Is Approved When:

  • ✔Hail impacts exceed test-square damage thresholds across 2 or more roof slopes.
  • ✔Widespread creased shingles cannot be repaired without damaging adjacent brittle tabs.
  • ✔The existing shingle style is discontinued (invoking NJ line-of-sight matching rules).
  • ✔Large tree branches have caused structural rafter and decking fracturing.
Your insurance carrier funds a full new roofing system with fresh 50-year warranty coverage.

Bridgewater Storm Restoration Permitting Rules

In New Jersey, minor storm repairs (such as replacing fewer than 25% of shingles on a facet or fixing a damaged pipe boot) are classified as ordinary maintenance and do NOT require building permits under the NJ UCC.

When storm damage is severe enough to justify a total insurance-funded roof replacement, a municipal building permit from the Bridgewater Township Code Enforcement department (505 Route 202/206) is required. ANSWERS TO COMMON QUESTIONS

Storm Damage FAQs

Straightforward answers to questions homeowners ask after storms in Bridgewater, NJ:

How do I know if my roof has storm damage if I cannot see any leaks? +

Most hail and wind damage does not produce immediate water leaks. Hailstones bruise shingles, fracturing the internal fiberglass core and knocking off granules, while wind breaks adhesive strips. Over the following months, UV rays deteriorate the exposed underlayment, resulting in delayed leaks. An on-roof forensic inspection reveals this damage early.

How long do I have to file a storm damage claim with my insurance company? +

Most New Jersey homeowner policies require reporting damage within 6 months to 1 year of the qualifying storm event. Because weather documentation becomes harder to verify over time, we recommend scheduling an inspection immediately following any major hail or high-wind storm.

Will filing a storm claim cause my homeowner insurance rates to increase? +

Insurance companies generally cannot raise rates on an individual homeowner for an "Act of God" weather event like hail or nor'easter gales. Rates are typically adjusted regionally across an entire ZIP code or county based on total weather losses, whether you personally file a claim or not.

What if my insurance adjuster denies my roof storm claim? +

Adjusters sometimes miss subtle hail bruising or creased shingle tabs during hasty inspections. You have the right to request a re-inspection with a different adjuster. Our team attends the second inspection with high-resolution test-square photos and manufacturer criteria to advocate for your full coverage.

What is New Jersey's "shingle matching" law? +

Under New Jersey insurance regulations, if damaged shingles are discontinued or cannot be reasonably matched in color and size with existing shingles, the insurance carrier must pay to replace the entire slope or adjoining planes to maintain a reasonably uniform appearance.

Can wind-damaged shingles just be glued back down? +

No. Once high winds lift and bend a shingle tab, the internal fiberglass mat creates a permanent crease line. Gluing the tab down leaves the cracked fiberglass unprotected, and driving rain will penetrate through the crease line in the next heavy rain.

Does emergency tarping prevent insurance reimbursement? +

No. In fact, standard insurance contracts legally require you to take reasonable emergency measures (like tarping) to mitigate further interior loss. Emergency tarping costs are invoiced directly as part of your covered storm claim.

Do storm repairs require permits in Bridgewater Township? +

Isolated shingle tab and flashing repairs do not require permits. If insurance approves a full roof replacement, permits from the Bridgewater Township Code Enforcement office (505 Route 202/206) are required. We handle all permit paperwork and town inspections.

How do you protect my property from nails and fallen debris? +

We wrap all landscaping, decks, and AC units in heavy canvas tarps before beginning tear-off. Following repair operations, our team runs commercial magnetic rollers three times around your lawn and driveway to collect every loose nail.

What warranty comes with storm damage roof repairs? +

All our storm repairs include a written watertight workmanship warranty, while full insurance-funded replacements qualify for manufacturer 50-year non-prorated system warranties covering materials and labor.
