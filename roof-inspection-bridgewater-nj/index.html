DIAGNOSTIC SCOPE

8 Critical Roof Conditions Our Inspections Uncover

Early detection prevents thousands of dollars in structural drywall, mold remediation, and wood rot damage:

Micro-Cracking & Granule Erosion

Thermal UV breakdown washes protective mineral granules away, exposing brittle fiberglass mats to rapid sun and rain deterioration.

Chimney & Step Flashing Failure

Cracked masonry mortar reglets, rusted flashing pans, and dried caulking allow wind-driven rain behind interior fireplace framing.

Dry-Rotted Pipe Boot Gaskets

Neoprene rubber collar seals around plumbing vent pipes crack after 7–10 years under UV exposure, causing silent trickling leaks.

Wind Uplift & Broken Seal Strips

Gale-force gusts break thermal adhesive bonds on shingle tabs, leaving them flapping loosely and vulnerable to blowing off entirely.

Valley Wear & Underlayment Leaks

High-volume water funnels through valleys. Deteriorated valley metal or cracked shingle weaves allow water underneath adjacent slopes.

Inadequate Attic Ventilation & Moisture

Blocked intake soffits trap hot, moist air in attics, baking shingles from below in summer and causing winter condensation mold.

Ice Damming Vulnerabilities Along Eaves

Missing or substandard ice and water shield membranes allow freeze-thaw ice dams to push meltwater backward under starter courses.

Latent Hail Bruising & Indentations

Severe thunderstorm hail fractures shingle fiberglass cores without tearing the surface, voiding warranties and creating future leaks.

METHODICAL WORKFLOW

Our 8-Step Roof Inspection Protocol

Every inspection follows our standardized forensic procedure to guarantee thoroughness, safety, and unbiased documentation:

01

Interior Ceiling & Rafter Audit

We check interior ceilings and enter the attic to inspect wooden rafters, sheathing underside, and insulation for moisture trails.

02

Physical On-Roof Walk & Shingle Tactile Test

Our inspector physically traverses slopes, testing shingle flexibility, granule bonding, nail depth, and thermal sealant adhesion.

03

Flashing, Mortar & Reglet Audit

We closely inspect chimney counter-flashing, skylight perimeter seals, headwall aprons, and step flashing behind exterior siding.

04

Valleys & Ice Shield Assessment

We examine closed-cut and open metal valleys, check eave drip edges, and verify proper ice and water shield overhang.

05

Penetration Boots & Collars

We check rubber plumbing pipe boots for UV dry rot, inspect furnace flue storm collars, and test exhaust fan dampers.

06

Attic Airflow & Ventilation Ratio

We measure Net Free Vent Area (NFVA) across ridge and intake soffit vents to ensure compliance with the 1:300 building code rule.

07

High-Definition Drone Aerial Imaging

We capture 4K aerial photography of steep, complex, or inaccessible roof facets to ensure 100% comprehensive visual coverage.

08

Digital Photo Report & Consultation

You receive an itemized PDF inspection report complete with annotated photos, remaining lifespan estimates, and prioritized recommendations.

TRANSPARENT PRICING

What Influences Roof Inspection Pricing?

While standard estimates are always complimentary, formal real estate and commercial certification reports have specific technical variables:

01

Inspection Purpose & Documentation Tier

A standard homeowner repair estimate vs. an official pre-purchase real estate certification letter for mortgage underwriters.

02

Roof Pitch & Slope Steepness

Steep roofs (8/12 pitch and higher) require specialized OSHA rope and harness safety gear, ladder staging, and toe-boards.

03

Building Height & Stories

Single-story ranch homes are fast to navigate, while 3-story custom colonials or estate properties require extended ladder work.

04

Square Footage & Facet Count

A simple 1,800 sq ft gable roof takes 45 minutes; a 5,000 sq ft estate with 18 hips, valleys, and dormers requires comprehensive auditing.

05

Attic Cavity Accessibility

Entering low-clearance or heavily insulated attics to inspect rafters, knee-walls, and soffit ventilation baffles.

06

Drone Aerial Photography

Deploying 4K camera drones to inspect slate, cedar, or ultra-steep roof planes that cannot be safely walked without tile damage.

07

Commercial Thermal Infrared Scans

Deploying FLIR thermal imaging cameras and taking calibrated core samples on commercial flat roofs to map wet insulation.

08

Turnaround Time & Urgency

Standard scheduling vs. same-day emergency inspection reports needed for impending real estate contingency deadlines.

MUNICIPAL COMPLIANCE

When Do Inspection Findings Trigger a Bridgewater Permit?

In Bridgewater Township and across New Jersey, conducting a roof inspection requires no municipal permits. Furthermore, under the Uniform Construction Code (UCC), minor ordinary maintenance (patching individual shingles, sealing flashing, or replacing pipe boots) is exempt from permit requirements.

However, if our inspection reveals that your roof has reached end of life and requires a complete re-roof, a building permit must be filed with Bridgewater Township Code Enforcement (505 Route 202/206). Bridgewater Roofer handles all permit filings and coordinates township building inspections.

ANSWERS TO COMMON QUESTIONS

Roof Inspection FAQs

Straightforward answers to questions homeowners, buyers, and agents ask about professional roof inspections in Bridgewater:

How does your roof inspection differ from a standard home inspector? +

Standard home inspectors are generalists who typically inspect the roof from the ground using binoculars or a quick drone shot due to liability limitations. Bridgewater Roofer's inspectors are licensed roofing professionals who physically walk the roof slopes, conduct tactile adhesion tests, examine flashing reglets up close, and enter the attic to check ventilation and rafters.

What is included in the 21-point roof inspection report? +

Our deliverable is a comprehensive digital PDF report that includes high-resolution annotated photographs of all roof facets, tactile shingle granule condition, chimney and step flashing analysis, rubber pipe boot status, gutter and valley drainage health, attic ventilation ratios, estimated remaining service life, and an itemized repair priority list.

How much does a professional roof inspection cost in Bridgewater? +

Standard roof assessments and repair/replacement estimates for homeowners are 100% complimentary. Formal written certifications for real estate transactions, insurance carrier disputes, or commercial thermal moisture scans involve a modest fee based on building square footage and pitch steepness, which is credited toward any subsequent repair work.

How quickly will I receive the written inspection report? +

We understand that real estate purchase contracts have tight inspection contingency deadlines. Our completed digital reports with annotated photos are typically delivered via email within 24 hours of completing the physical on-site audit.

Do you actually climb onto the roof during the inspection? +

Yes. Whenever weather and pitch conditions permit safe access, our certified technicians climb directly onto the roof. Physically touching shingles, testing sealant adhesion, and checking flashing seams reveals critical defects that are completely invisible from the ground.

Can your inspection report help with my homeowners insurance claim? +

Absolutely. Following nor'easters, severe wind events, or hailstorms, our forensic inspection documents the exact date of loss, counts hail impacts per 100 sq ft test square, photographs fractured fiberglass mats, and provides the detailed engineering documentation required by insurance adjusters to approve claims.

Why is an attic inspection necessary during a roof audit? +

The underside of your roof tells half the story. Inspecting the attic reveals rotted plywood sheathing, hidden water stains along rafters, mold from inadequate ventilation, and blocked soffit vents that cook shingles from underneath and cause premature failure.

How often should a residential roof be inspected in New Jersey? +

We recommend a professional inspection every two years for roofs under 10 years old, and annually for roofs older than 12 years. Additionally, an inspection should always be performed immediately following any major severe storm or hurricane event.

Will your inspector try to pressure me into a full roof replacement? +

Never. Bridgewater Roofer operates with an honest, repair-first philosophy. Over 60% of our inspections result in either a clean bill of health or minor preventative maintenance recommendations (such as resealing flashing or replacing a pipe collar). We only recommend replacement when shingles have genuinely exceeded their useful service life.

Are your inspectors licensed and insured in New Jersey? +

Yes. Bridgewater Roofer is a fully licensed New Jersey Home Improvement Contractor (#13VH13322100) carrying comprehensive general liability and full workers' compensation coverage. Our inspectors hold factory master credentials from GAF and CertainTeed.
