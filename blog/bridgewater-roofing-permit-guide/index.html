Do You Need a Permit to Replace a Roof in Bridgewater, NJ? (Permit & Code Guide)

If you are planning a roof replacement in Bridgewater or Somerset County, understanding township building permits and New Jersey Uniform Construction Code (NJ UCC) regulations is vital. Key Takeaways for Homeowners

  • Yes, Permits Are Mandatory: Bridgewater Township Code Enforcement requires a building permit for all full roof replacements, re-roofs, and major structural decking repairs.
  • Minor Repairs Exemption: Patching minor leaks or replacing a small cluster of storm-damaged shingles (under 25% of the roof plane) is classified as ordinary maintenance and does not require a permit.
  • Two-Layer Maximum: New Jersey UCC Chapter 15 prohibits installing more than two layers of shingles. If two layers already exist, the roof must be stripped to the bare decking.
  • Ice Barrier Mandate: Code requires self-adhering polymer modified bitumen ice barrier installed along all eaves to at least 24 inches inside the interior heated wall plane.
  • Contractor Licensing: By NJ law, only New Jersey Division of Consumer Affairs registered Home Improvement Contractors (HIC) carrying active general liability insurance can pull permits on your behalf.

Bridgewater Permit Requirements: When Is a Permit Needed?

Under the New Jersey Uniform Construction Code (N.J.A.C. 5:23) and the International Residential Code (IRC NJ Edition), residential roofing work is classified under specific tiers of municipal oversight:

Permit Required:

  • Complete tear-off and replacement of asphalt shingles, metal panels, or flat membrane systems.
  • Installation of a second overlay layer over an existing single shingle layer.
  • Structural decking replacement exceeding 25% of the total roof surface or rafter structural modifications.

Permit NOT Required (Ordinary Maintenance): Minor spot repairs replacing individual damaged shingles, resealing loose flashing around a vent pipe, or re-caulking gutters generally qualify as ordinary maintenance under N.J.A.C. 5:23-2.7 and do not require municipal permits.

Permit Application Process & Required Municipal Forms

Applying for a roofing permit in Bridgewater involves submitting specific standard state construction forms to the Construction Official at 100 Commons Way:

Key NJ UCC Roofing Codes Every Homeowner Must Know

Inspectors in Somerset County evaluate every replacement against strict performance standards:

1. Section R905.1.2 — Ice Barrier Requirement: In areas with a history of ice damming (all of Somerset County), an ice barrier consisting of at least two layers of underlayment cemented together or a self-adhering polymer-modified bitumen sheet must be installed from the eave's edge to a point at least 24 inches inside the exterior wall line.

2. Section R905.2.8.5 — Drip Edge Installation: Drip edge metal must be provided at eaves and gables of shingle roofs. The drip edge must overlap the underlayment by at least 2 inches at eaves and extend down the fascia board by at least 1/4 inch.

3. Section R806.1 — Attic Ventilation Ratios: The code mandates minimum net free ventilating area of 1/150 of the attic floor space, or 1/300 when balanced between ridge exhaust and soffit intake. Failure to maintain balanced airflow voids shingle manufacturer warranties.

Municipal Inspection Steps: What the Inspector Checks

Once your roof replacement is finished, Bridgewater Township Code Enforcement conducts a required final inspection before issuing a Certificate of Approval:

  • Flashing & Counter-Flashing: Verification that chimney counter-flashing is mortared or sealed and wall step-flashing is properly seated beneath siding.
  • Ventilation & Exhaust Integrity: Ensuring that bathroom vent ducts and furnace flues remain securely connected and exhaust freely outside.
  • Ground Cleanup & Nail Sweeping: Confirmation that job debris, old shingles, and stray nails have been fully cleared from walkways, driveways, and lawns.

Contractor vs. Homeowner Responsibilities

Never Pull a "Homeowner Permit" for a Contractor!

If a contractor asks you to pull the permit as a "homeowner doing your own work," decline immediately. Contractors who make this request often lack an active NJ Home Improvement Contractor license, carry insufficient workers' compensation insurance, or are attempting to dodge state liability laws.
